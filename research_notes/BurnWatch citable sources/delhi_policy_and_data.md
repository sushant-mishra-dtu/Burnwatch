# Delhi open-burning policy, evidence and enforcement

Research checked 2026-10-10. Access dates below are all 2026-10-10. Sources are ordered by evidentiary priority: Delhi/CAQM/DPCC primary documents first, then peer-reviewed research, then a clearly labelled news report where the underlying primary record was not located.

## Official prohibition and winter air-pollution actions

### Takeaway

Open burning of garbage, leaves, waste, plastic, rubber and similar material is prohibited in Delhi under the NGT directions adopted by GNCTD. Current CAQM GRAP directions separately require strict enforcement against open biomass and municipal-solid-waste burning, vigilance at landfill/dumpsites, winter alternatives such as electric heaters, and reporting through Green Delhi/311/SAMEER channels.

### Cited Findings

- **Delhi Environment Department order (20 October 2015) implementing NGT O.A. 21/2014.** The order says the NGT directed prohibition of burning “any kind of garbage leaves, waste, plastic, rubber, self moulding compound and such other materials in the open.” It also records an instantaneous **Rs 5,000** compensation for a person found burning such material. Source title: *Order: Prohibition/compensation for open burning*; publisher: Department of Environment, Government of NCT of Delhi; publication date shown on scanned order: **20-10-2015**; URL: [open_burning.pdf](https://environment.delhi.gov.in/sites/default/files/environment/circulars-orders/open_burning.pdf); accessed **2026-10-10**. Verbatim quote: “has directed prohibition of burning of any kind of garbage leaves, waste, plastic, rubber, self moulding compound and such other materials in the open.”

- **Delhi Environment Department campaign page.** The page repeats the NGT compensation rule and states that Section 37 of the Air Act, 1981 can bring imprisonment of 1.5–6 years plus a continuing fine up to Rs 5,000 per day for failure to comply with a Section 31(A) direction. Source title: *Burning of leaves*; publisher: Department of Environment, Government of NCT of Delhi; publication date: **not stated** (page last updated **05-10-2026**); URL: [Burning of leaves](https://environment.delhi.gov.in/environment/burning-leaves); accessed **2026-10-10**. Verbatim quote: “would not be less than one year and six months, but which may extend to six years with fine.” Treat this as a statement of the page’s legal summary; consult the Act/order for legal advice.

- **CAQM Direction No. 104, revised GRAP schedule.** The current schedule dated **29-09-2026** requires NCR authorities, including Delhi agencies, to strictly enforce the prohibition on open biomass and municipal-solid-waste burning, impose maximum environmental compensation under NGT orders, and maintain vigilance so landfill/dumpsite burning incidents do not occur. Source title: *Direction No. 104 — Revised Schedule of the Graded Response Action Plan (GRAP) for NCR*; publisher: Commission for Air Quality Management in NCR and Adjoining Areas (CAQM); publication date: **29-09-2026**; URL: [Direction No. 104 PDF](https://environment.delhi.gov.in/sites/default/files/environment/universal/direction-104_revised_grap_schedule_sep_2026.pdf); accessed **2026-10-10**. Verbatim quote: “Strictly enforce prohibition on open burning of bio-mass and municipal solid waste.”

- **CAQM Direction No. 104 winter measures.** The same current schedule assigns Resident Welfare Associations and municipal heads a winter role: avoid open biomass/MSW burning, including by providing electric heaters to security, sanitation, horticulture and other staff. Source: same [Direction No. 104 PDF](https://environment.delhi.gov.in/sites/default/files/environment/universal/direction-104_revised_grap_schedule_sep_2026.pdf), CAQM, **29-09-2026**, accessed **2026-10-10**. Verbatim quote: “staff engaged in security, sanitation, horticulture and other miscellaneous services to avoid open biomass/ MSW burning during winters.”

- **CAQM Direction No. 104 reporting channel.** The current GRAP public-advice section tells residents to report air-polluting activity using the 311 App, Green Delhi App and SAMEER App. Source: same [Direction No. 104 PDF](https://environment.delhi.gov.in/sites/default/files/environment/universal/direction-104_revised_grap_schedule_sep_2026.pdf), CAQM, **29-09-2026**, accessed **2026-10-10**. Verbatim quote: “Report air polluting activities through 311 App, Green Delhi App, SAMEER App etc.”

- **Earlier CAQM GRAP schedule (Direction No. 75).** The revised July 2023 schedule, effective for the winter framework from 01-10-2023, used the same policy architecture: stringent prohibition on open biomass/MSW burning, maximum environmental compensation, and vigilance at landfill/dumpsites. Source title: *Direction No. 75 — Implementation of GRAP for NCR including NCT of Delhi (Revised in July, 2023)*; publisher: CAQM; publication date: **27-07-2023**; URL: [Direction No. 75 PDF](https://environment.delhi.gov.in/sites/default/files/environment/universal/direction_no._75.pdf); accessed **2026-10-10**. Verbatim quote: “Stringently enforce prohibition on open burning of biomass and municipal solid waste.”

- **Delhi Environment Department public-awareness implementation.** The GNCTD page records three half-day workshops in 2017–2018 for North, South and East MCD areas, involving municipal sanitary/horticulture officials, RWAs and educational institutions. Source title: *Burning of leaves*; publisher: Department of Environment, GNCTD; date: **14-06-2017, 14-09-2017 and 01-02-2018 workshops** (page last updated 05-10-2026); URL: [Burning of leaves](https://environment.delhi.gov.in/environment/burning-leaves); accessed **2026-10-10**. Verbatim quote: “Environment Department, GNCTD organized three half day workshops ... on ‘Prohibition on open burning of any kind of material’.”

### Inferences

- The policy has two layers: a standing prohibition/compensation regime rooted in NGT O.A. 21/2014 and a seasonal CAQM GRAP implementation regime that adds winter operational instructions, inter-agency vigilance and public reporting channels.
- “Biomass burning” in GRAP is broader than municipal garbage burning: it can include leaves, wood and other biomass. Do not describe the CAQM rule as limited to household garbage.

### Gaps

- I did not locate, in the searched official pages, a public time series showing how many Rs 5,000 compensation notices were actually issued or collected under the 2015/2025 authorization orders.
- A legal summary on a campaign page is not a substitute for the full Air Act, NGT order or case-specific legal advice.

## Quantitative contribution, incidents and complaints

### Takeaway

The strongest current quantitative evidence is source-apportionment, not a count of Delhi municipal open-burning events. DPCC’s official 2023 supersite report gives a winter-average **24% biomass-burning** contribution to PM2.5 (within and outside Delhi), while a 2024 peer-reviewed Delhi study estimates **24% from residential heating and waste burning** and **23% from fresh paddy-residue burning**. Neither figure should be presented as “24% of Delhi PM2.5 comes from open garbage burning”: the categories and geographic scopes are wider.

### Cited Findings

- **DPCC/IIT/TERI/Airshed official supersite report.** The winter-season average for **01-11-2022 to 02-03-2023** identifies biomass burning as **24% of PM2.5**, explicitly “within and outside Delhi”; SIA is 32% and vehicles 17%. Source title: *Real-Time Source Apportionment and Forecasting for Advance Air Pollution Management in Delhi — Winter Season Report (Revised)*; publisher: Delhi Pollution Control Committee, Delhi Government, with IIT Kanpur, IIT Delhi, TERI and Airshed; publication date: **September 2023**; URL: [DPCC report PDF](https://www.dpcc.delhigovt.nic.in/uploads/news/819b9fef9cf4a2a574a10d3bbc421cfd.pdf); accessed **2026-10-10**. Verbatim quote (23 words): “The average of winter season’s source apportionment shows SIA (32 percent) and biomass burning (24 percent; within and outside Delhi) contribute the most.” **Scope warning:** this is all biomass burning in the model, not a measurement of open garbage burning alone.

- **DPCC official report: crop-residue subset.** For **01–25 November 2022**, the report estimates mean crop-residue (“parali”) contribution at **22%** of Delhi PM2.5, exceeding 35% on certain days. Source: same [DPCC report PDF](https://www.dpcc.delhigovt.nic.in/uploads/news/819b9fef9cf4a2a574a10d3bbc421cfd.pdf); DPCC/Delhi Government and consortium; **September 2023**; accessed **2026-10-10**. Verbatim quote (22 words): “In this period mean contribution of parali burning was 22%, however, on certain days, it was very high and exceeded 35 percent.” **Scope warning:** this is regional agricultural-residue burning transported into Delhi, not local open garbage burning.

- **Peer-reviewed source apportionment (2022 Delhi measurements).** A PMF study using 2022 measurements at the India Meteorological Department’s Mausam Bhawan site in Central Delhi estimated **23% of PM2.5 from fresh paddy-residue burning** and **24% from residential heating and waste burning**. Source title: *Biomass-burning sources control ambient particulate matter, but traffic and industrial sources control volatile organic compound (VOC) emissions and secondary-pollutant formation during extreme pollution events in Delhi*; publisher: *Atmospheric Chemistry and Physics*; publication date: **18-09-2024**; DOI/URL: [10.5194/acp-24-10279-2024](https://doi.org/10.5194/acp-24-10279-2024); accessed **2026-10-10**. Verbatim quote (23 words): “Burning of fresh paddy residue, as well as residential heating and waste burning, contributed ... PM2.5 levels (23 % and 24 %, respectively).” **Scope warning:** the “residential heating and waste burning” factor is the closest peer-reviewed quantitative analogue to local waste burning, but it is not an open-garbage-only estimate and is not a Delhi annual average.

- **Peer-reviewed Delhi study without a single percentage.** Bangar et al. identify biomass burning, solid-waste burning, vehicles, coal combustion, secondary aerosols and industry as source classes in post-monsoon Delhi, and attribute biomass-burning influence largely to long-range agricultural-residue transport. Source title: *Elemental Characteristics and Source-Apportionment of PM2.5 During the Post-monsoon Season in Delhi, India*; publisher: *Frontiers in Sustainable Cities*; publication date: **12-04-2021**; DOI/URL: [10.3389/frsc.2021.648551](https://doi.org/10.3389/frsc.2021.648551); accessed **2026-10-10**. Verbatim quote (21 words): “The source involving biomass burning contributed largely to the PM2.5 in post-monsoon season through long-range transport of large-scale agriculture-residue burning emissions.” This supports the mechanism but does not supply an open-burning percentage suitable for a README headline.

- **Secondary incident figure (use only with attribution and caveat).** *Down To Earth* reported Delhi Fire Services records of **4,933 garbage/rubbish-burning incidents through 12-12-2024**, versus **3,511 in 2023**. Source title: *Delhi burns its waste openly, chokes itself more*; publisher: *Down To Earth*; publication date: **10-02-2026**; URL: [Down To Earth article](https://www.downtoearth.org.in/air/delhi-burns-its-waste-openly-chokes-itself-more); accessed **2026-10-10**. Verbatim quote (21 words): “In 2024, Delhi Fire Services recorded 4,933 incidents of garbage/rubbish burning up to December 12, an increase from 3,511 in 2023.” **Verification status: not independently verified.** I did not find the underlying Delhi Fire Services table or release; do not present this as an official primary-source statistic without obtaining that record.

### Inferences

- For a citable README/video, the defensible wording is: “Delhi’s official supersite report estimated biomass burning at 24% of winter PM2.5, including sources within and outside Delhi,” followed by the scope caveat. A separate sentence can cite the peer-reviewed 2024 study’s 24% residential-heating/waste-burning factor.
- It is scientifically and rhetorically unsafe to turn either 24% number into “open burning causes 24% of Delhi pollution.” The reported categories include transported agricultural residue and/or residential heating, and one study’s source is a single central-Delhi receptor/time window.

### Gaps

- I found no primary Delhi Government/DPCC/CPCB public dataset that quantifies **open garbage/leaf burning alone** as a percentage of Delhi PM2.5.
- I found no primary Green Delhi complaint export giving open-burning complaint counts, response times, closure rates or enforcement outcomes as of 2026-10-10.
- The 4,933/3,511 incident numbers remain a news-reported lead, not a verified primary statistic. Do not use them as a project claim unless Delhi Fire Services confirms the category definition, period and source table.

## How enforcement currently works: field authorities and complaint apps

### Takeaway

The current documented enforcement chain combines delegated field officers who can levy compensation, mandatory reporting to DPCC, municipal/CAQM GRAP responsibilities, landfill vigilance, and citizen reports through Green Delhi/311/SAMEER. Green Delhi’s public portal confirms citizen participation and links an ICCC/Enforcement App, but the public pages do not expose a complete complaint workflow or outcome dataset.

### Cited Findings

- **Current GNCTD authorization order (09-12-2025).** The order authorizes **Deputy Tehsildar and above in District Administration; Sanitary Inspector and above in the Municipal Corporation of Delhi; and Sanitary Inspector and above in NDMC** to levy compensation for open burning. Source title: *Authorisation order on open burning control*; publisher: Department of Environment, GNCTD; order date on scanned PDF: **09-12-2025**; listing page date: **12-10-2025**; URLs: [listing page](https://environment.delhi.gov.in/node/14177) and [order PDF](https://environment.delhi.gov.in/sites/default/files/environment/universal/1000141129.pdf); accessed **2026-10-10**. Verbatim quote (15 words): “Sanitary Inspector and above of New Delhi Municipal Council, are hereby authorized to levy compensation.”

- **Current GNCTD authorization order: amount and accountability.** The same order sets the NGT compensation at **Rs 5,000**, directs deposit of collected compensation with DPCC, and requires designated officers to submit a monthly action-taken report to DPCC. Source: [order PDF](https://environment.delhi.gov.in/sites/default/files/environment/universal/1000141129.pdf); Department of Environment, GNCTD; **09-12-2025**; accessed **2026-10-10**. Verbatim quote (18 words): “Monthly action taken report to be submitted by the designated officers to Delhi Pollution Control Committee, without fail.”

- **CAQM/GRAP inter-agency field responsibility.** Direction 104 places the open-burning prohibition and landfill/dumpsite vigilance under the responsible NCR pollution-control and municipal authorities, including CPCB, DPCC/SPCB leadership and municipal commissioners/heads. Source title: *Direction No. 104 — Revised Schedule of GRAP for NCR*; publisher: CAQM; publication date: **29-09-2026**; URL: [Direction No. 104 PDF](https://environment.delhi.gov.in/sites/default/files/environment/universal/direction-104_revised_grap_schedule_sep_2026.pdf); accessed **2026-10-10**. Verbatim quote (16 words): “Strict vigil to ensure that there are no burning incidents in the landfill sites / dumpsites.”

- **Citizen complaint channels in current official direction.** CAQM’s current public-advice text specifically names **311 App, Green Delhi App and SAMEER App** as routes for reporting air-polluting activities. Source: [Direction No. 104 PDF](https://environment.delhi.gov.in/sites/default/files/environment/universal/direction-104_revised_grap_schedule_sep_2026.pdf); CAQM; **29-09-2026**; accessed **2026-10-10**. Verbatim quote (13 words): “Report air polluting activities through 311 App, Green Delhi App, SAMEER App etc.”

- **Green Delhi portal.** The GNCTD portal describes itself as a citizen participation platform, says it empowers Delhi citizens to suggest/report/pledge, and links an “ICCC/Enforcement App” download. Source title: *Green Delhi Portal | Jan Bhagidari*; publisher: Government of NCT of Delhi; publication date: **not stated**; URL: [greendelhi.nic.in](https://www.greendelhi.nic.in/); accessed **2026-10-10**. Verbatim quote (17 words): “Empowering 2 crore citizens of NCT of Delhi to suggest, report, and pledge for a cleaner tomorrow.” The portal page itself does not publish open-burning complaint counts, response SLAs or closure data.

- **Older but explicit operational model.** GNCTD’s *Burning of leaves* page says 2017–2018 workshops involved MCD sanitary inspectors/assistant sanitary inspectors and horticulture wings together with RWAs and schools/colleges. Source: [Burning of leaves](https://environment.delhi.gov.in/environment/burning-leaves); Department of Environment, GNCTD; workshop dates **14-06-2017, 14-09-2017, 01-02-2018**; page last updated 05-10-2026; accessed **2026-10-10**. Verbatim quote (19 words): “The officials from North MCD, South MCD and East MCD i.e. SI/ASI and horticulture wing participated in the workshops.”

### Inferences

- A practical description of current enforcement is: a citizen submits a location/photo report through an official app; the responsible municipal/field authority investigates; authorized officers can levy the NGT-linked compensation; and designated officers report action to DPCC. The sources establish the channels and authority, but not a guaranteed response time or an end-to-end app workflow.
- BurnWatch can accurately position itself as an additional visual detection/alert layer that can supply evidence to existing authority and complaint workflows. It should not claim to replace the authorized officers or to issue fines.

### Gaps

- The Green Delhi public portal does not state which department receives each open-burning complaint, how a complaint is routed, what proof is required, or the current median response/closure time.
- No public official dataset located during this search reports open-burning complaints by app, district, month, disposition or fines collected.
- CAQM’s 2026 “Operation Clean Air” inspection material was discoverable in search results, but the direct CAQM PDF endpoint returned an HTML error in this environment; I therefore do not rely on unverified inspection counts or enforcement outcomes from it.

## Source-use checklist for README/video

- Safe headline: **“Open burning of garbage and biomass is prohibited in Delhi; current CAQM GRAP directions require strict enforcement and landfill vigilance.”** Cite the 2025 GNCTD authorization order plus CAQM Direction 104.
- Safe quantitative line: **“DPCC’s winter 2022–23 supersite report estimated biomass burning at 24% of PM2.5, including sources within and outside Delhi.”** Keep the scope qualifier on-screen or in the README.
- Safe peer-reviewed line: **“A 2024 peer-reviewed Delhi study estimated 23% of PM2.5 from fresh paddy-residue burning and 24% from residential heating and waste burning during its 2022 campaign.”** Do not label these as open-garbage-only shares.
- Safe enforcement line: **“GNCTD authorizes district and municipal sanitary officers to levy Rs 5,000 compensation and requires monthly action reports to DPCC; residents are directed to 311, Green Delhi and SAMEER apps.”**
- Avoid: “open burning contributes exactly X% of Delhi PM2.5,” “Delhi recorded X official complaints,” or “every Green Delhi report is answered within Y minutes.” Those claims were not verified from primary public records.
